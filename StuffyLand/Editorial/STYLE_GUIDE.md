# Stuffy Land style guide

Stuffy Land is a funny, emotionally warm fantasy world built around Opal, her brother, her sloth lovey, and their plush friends. The app should feel like opening a treasured illustrated book inside her galaxy tent. This guide applies to prose, interactive scenes, illustrations, interface copy, and future additions.

## Source authority

Use the supplied expanded recovered archive for character facts and relationships, and the three complete transcript stories for voice and established events. The two uploaded DOCX files are byte-identical; they are one source, not two collections. The original transcript explicitly warns that older story wording is unavailable. The source art contains mistakes the user previously corrected, so pictures do not override the written character bible.

See `UNIVERSE.md` for canon and `story-provenance.json` for each app story's origin. Keep complete recovered prose in `source/`; keep newly written adaptations in `stories/`. Never describe a reconstructed story as a recovered original. New characters, artifacts, names, and events in adaptations are editorial additions, not facts recovered from missing originals.

## Narrative voice

Use a close third-person viewpoint centered on Opal. Let her notice details, make a plan, sometimes misunderstand, and decide what to do next. Narration is concrete, readable aloud, and lightly playful. Alternate immersive passages with quick dialogue; do not make every sentence a separate dramatic reveal. The complete Giggle Games text is retained as supplied, including its distinctive short beats.

Humor comes from character: Langston's ambitious announcements, suspicious snack logic, theatrical confidence, and ridiculous improvisation; Finley's observations; Axey's enthusiasm; Lolo's patient replies. Langston is protective, clever, heroic, and much taller than Opal. Trolly does not mean cruel. Keep The Annoyings partnership with Frosted Kitty.

Lolo is a female gray-and-tan sloth lovey, never a second girl or a bear. Give her emotional insight that changes what Opal understands or chooses. Axey, her best friend, should actively investigate and explore rather than vanish from the plot.

Let people name feelings and needs without turning dialogue into a lesson worksheet. Show apologies followed by repair. Compassion can explain a villain's behavior without excusing harm or making immediate forgiveness compulsory. Rest, asking for help, and trying again can be brave choices.

## Story shape

Open with a recognizable activity in the bed, galaxy tent, village, or a connected imaginary place. Introduce a specific oddity, follow clues or a challenge, complicate the first plan, and resolve the problem through observation, teamwork, humor, or understanding. Land in a cozy moment with an earned character joke.

Opal's gemstone amplifies collective effort or reveals a useful connection; it cannot erase the puzzle automatically. Give other characters a needed job. Powers retain their limits, especially effects that work only on villains and shields that protect heroes only. No realistic injury, frightening punishment, or practical instructions for dangerous substances.

Use a title, no added chapter headings unless requested, and finish new linear stories with “The End.” App pages are silent reading sections, not newly invented chapters. Read-aloud duration depends on voice speed: the newly expanded shorter stories are about 5–7 minutes, and the supplied Giggle Games is longer. Future 12–15 minute requests need more developed scenes, not slower narration or inflated estimates.

## Interactive adventures

The reader helps Opal decide. Phrase choices as specific actions: “Study the moving star” or “Ask Duke what keeps him awake.” Use two or three plausible actions in ordinary scenes. Endings can have a wider set of visible options with clear prerequisite hints.

Branch into distinct scenes, assign meaningful roles, and remember decisions with flags. Later prose should acknowledge discovered clues and promises. Curiosity, Courage, and Friendship belong to the existing engine, but avoid presenting kindness or rest as a ranked moral exam. Stuffy Land shows ending memories without an outcome-rank label.

Keep the app's rewindable detours and multiple endings. Detours are short comic outcomes with explicit reasons to try again. Endings must resolve the immediate situation and describe what remains to fix. At least one finish stays available on every playable path. A more complete resolution should require preparation rather than appear for everyone automatically.

Pillow-Fort keeps Opal resting as mission commander on every route. Snore-Snatcher restores the stolen sounds; the longer-term outcome depends on whether the group also understands and addresses Duke's need for quiet. Nobody has to surrender their voice to be kind.

## Visual identity

Use the galaxy tent as the visual home: plum fabric, lavender pillows, warm gold stars, amber lanterns, and cream book pages. Keep magic concentrated in the illustrations. Reading areas remain calm and high contrast.

Avoid neon gradients, constant sparkle animation, space-console labels, tiny uppercase paragraphs, and crowded character collages behind text.

The app icon is Lolo above an open lavender book with the Friendship Star. It uses one recognizable companion instead of squeezing the entire cast into an icon. Store a square, opaque 1024px icon; let iOS apply the external mask. The home illustration shows one Opal holding one Lolo inside the tent. New generated art is an interpretation, not an unaltered source image. The art prompt record lives in `../Design/ART_DIRECTION.md`.

## Illustration continuity

For a focused scene, include only the specified cast, once each. A complete ensemble means all 31 Aphmau cats plus the main cast. Never add filler animals or duplicate a character to fill space. If dogs appear, use exactly Finley and Stella: both fluffy white doodle girls; Stella is slightly smaller and calmer. Human Opal has shoulder-length dirty-blonde curls; Langston is visibly taller and bigger. Coatl's shell belongs on his back and danger buttons on its underside. Lolo is an actual slender sloth lovey.

All Aphmau plushes remain visibly cats even when dressed as other animals, food, or fantasy creatures. Keep the Dragon Couple together: entirely purple boy and dark-blue girl with light-blue gem. Aaron-y and Knight Kitty must be distinct. Preserve Caterpillar Kitty's rainbow segments, Chicky Chick's eggshell shorts and ribbon, C's two bows, and Princess Kitty versus Love Kitty. Use a tiered composition and readable labels only when the requested illustration calls for them. Different story illustrations should show different events.

Do not reproduce the unrelated “The Lost Realm” poster in the attachment as Stuffy Land art. Do not import the source's known duplicate-human and incorrect-label pictures into app covers. Source image 1 is a child's blue-and-black character drawing; preserve it as reference, not an invented cast photograph.

## Interface language

Use “Story shelf,” “Read or listen,” “Choose an adventure,” “Read to me,” “Back to stories,” and “Try another way.” Shelves explain the two formats before opening. Keep progress local and offer the reader's existing text size, bookmark, voice speed, pause, resume, and stop controls.

Keep canon and provenance information accessible from the shelf's story notes. The original Giggle Games remains explicitly a recovered original; reconstructed books have a visible adaptation label. Publishing claims, build availability, and audience classification must reflect actual verified app state, independently of this creative guide.
