Opal and the Great Snore-Snatcher

It was a beautiful afternoon in Stuffy Land. The sun was shining, the flowers were humming little songs, and Langston was attempting to balance six cookies on his forehead.

“Seven is my personal record,” he announced.

“Your record is six,” Opal reminded him.

“That's why I'm attempting seven.”

Lolo was curled up beside Opal, watching the cookie experiment with great interest. Axey was practicing explorer jumps across a row of cushions, while Finley and Stella, the two fluffy white doodle girls, were chasing a butterfly made of tiny golden stars.

Everything was peaceful.

A little TOO peaceful, actually.

Because deep beneath the Whispering Woods, something was stirring.

Something enormous.

Something sneaky.

Something that had been asleep for approximately four hundred years.

And it was VERY grumpy.

His name was Duke Dozington, the Snore-Snatcher.

Duke Dozington was a strange creature shaped like a giant purple pillow. He had two tiny legs, four enormous arms, a nightcap with a dangling bell, and a mustache that curled up like two sleepy caterpillars.

He had a special power.

He could steal the sounds of sleeping creatures.

Their snores, their sleepy sighs, their little murmurs, and even their happy dreams could be sucked into his enormous pillow-shaped belly.

And he had just awakened with a terrible idea.

“If everyone in Stuffy Land is asleep,” he grumbled, “then nobody will make noise! No singing! No laughing! No dancing! And most importantly, nobody will interrupt MY naps!”

He rubbed his enormous belly.

“Excellent. Time to collect some snores.”

He climbed onto his floating pillow and drifted toward Stuffy Land.

The first strange thing happened at the village square.

Cherryberry was telling a joke.

“Why did the kitty bring a ladder to the bakery?”

Everyone waited.

“Because she wanted a HIGHER ROLL!”

Nothing happened.

Nobody laughed.

Not because the joke was bad.

Because nobody could hear it!

Cherryberry's mouth moved, but no sound came out.

She blinked.

She tried again.

Still nothing.

Then a giant purple cloud floated over the village.

A strange sucking noise came from inside it.

“SHLUUUUUUUURP!”

Every sound in the square disappeared.

The bells stopped ringing. The fountains stopped splashing. Even the wind seemed to go quiet.

Axey bounced into the square.

“HELLO, EVERYBODY!”

His mouth moved.

No sound.

He bounced again.

Still nothing.

Then Langston came running.

He was shouting something very dramatic, but nobody could hear him.

Opal watched his enormous arms wave around.

She turned to Lolo.

“What's happening?”

Lolo shook her head.

Then she noticed something floating above the village.

A giant purple pillow with a mustache.

On top sat a creature wearing a nightcap.

He was holding a giant vacuum cleaner shaped like a trumpet.

A sign hung around his neck:

“DUKE DOZINGTON — PROFESSIONAL SLEEP ENFORCER.”

Opal pointed.

“THAT MUST BE THE BAD GUY!”

Duke Dozington waved cheerfully.

Then he pressed a button on his vacuum trumpet.

“SHLUUUUUUUURP!”

A little golden cloud floated out of the village and disappeared into his belly.

Duke patted his stomach.

“AHHH. A delicious collection of giggles.”

He burped.

A tiny musical note escaped.

Then he fell asleep for three seconds.

When he woke up, he continued flying.

Opal gathered everyone inside the galaxy tent.

“Okay. We need to figure out what he's doing.”

Lolo held Opal's paw.

“I think he's stealing sounds.”

Axey nodded.

He opened his mouth and pointed at it, then pointed toward the purple cloud.

Finley sniffed the floor and discovered a trail of tiny purple feathers. Stella followed the trail to a strange little machine Duke had accidentally dropped.

It was a small metal box with a glowing screen.

Opal picked it up.

The screen displayed:

“SNORE-SNATCHER 3000”

Below that were three buttons:

- GIGGLES- SONGS- SNORES

And one enormous red button:

“EVERYTHING!”

Langston stared at it.

“That's a lot of buttons.”

Opal examined the machine.

“Maybe he needs all the sounds to power something.”

Lolo looked at the screen.

“There's a little picture of a giant pillow.”

Axey pointed toward the forest.

Perhaps the machine was connected to Duke's floating pillow.

Opal nodded.

“We need to find his hideout.”

Langston stood up.

“FINALLY! A MISSION FOR A TROLL OF MY CALIBER!”

He flexed.

His cookie tower fell off his forehead.

One cookie landed in his hand.

He ate it.

“MISSION SNACK.”

The friends followed the purple feather trail into the Whispering Woods.

They walked past the glowing mushrooms, crossed the wobbly bridge, and climbed a hill covered in sleepy-looking flowers.

At the top stood a gigantic castle made entirely of pillows.

It had pillow towers, pillow walls, pillow windows, and a giant pillow-shaped drawbridge.

A sign above the entrance read:

“DOZINGTON'S DREAM DUNGEON.”

“That's definitely his house,” Opal whispered.

Langston stared at the castle.

“It's beautiful.”

“You like it?” Axey asked.

“I've always wanted a castle made of pillows.”

He took one step forward.

“FWOOOOOMP!”

His foot sank into the pillow ground.

Then another step.

“FWOOOOOMP!”

He sank up to his knees.

“THIS CASTLE IS EATING ME!”

Finley and Stella tugged on his cape.

Coatl helped pull him free.

Langston emerged covered in feathers.

“I have been attacked by architecture.”

Opal giggled.

Then she noticed something important.

The castle wasn't just made of pillows.

Every pillow glowed with a tiny golden light.

Inside each one was a stolen sound.

A little laugh.

A song.

A sleepy sigh.

Even the sound of a dog barking.

Duke Dozington was keeping them all!

The friends sneaked toward the castle entrance.

But Duke Dozington was waiting.

He floated above the doorway, wearing fuzzy slippers and holding his trumpet vacuum.

“WELL, WELL, WELL. VISITORS!”

Opal stepped forward.

“Give everyone their sounds back!”

Duke laughed.

It was a deep, rumbling laugh that sounded like someone shaking a bag of marbles.

“NEVER! I have collected the greatest collection of sounds in the entire world!”

“Why?” Opal asked.

“BECAUSE I LOVE QUIET!”

“But you can't steal everyone's sounds just because you like quiet.”

Duke crossed his arms.

“I CAN AND I DID!”

Then he pointed his trumpet vacuum at the group.

“SHLUUUUUUUURP!”

A purple beam shot toward them.

Everyone ducked.

Langston wasn't fast enough.

The beam hit his pumpkin helmet.

Suddenly, the helmet began making a snoring noise.

“HOOOOONK-SHOOOOO.”

Langston stared at it.

“Why is my helmet snoring?”

Duke giggled.

“BECAUSE I STOLE YOUR SNORE!”

Langston looked horrified.

“MY SNORE?”

He turned to Opal.

“I DON'T EVEN SNORE!”

Lolo raised an eyebrow.

Everyone looked at him.

Langston sighed.

“Fine. A little.”

Duke Dozington opened the castle doors.

Inside was a gigantic machine.

It had spinning wheels, glowing pipes, and a huge glass chamber.

At the center was a giant purple pillow shaped like a heart.

Duke floated proudly beside it.

“This is my Ultimate Quiet Machine! When it is fully powered, every sound in Stuffy Land will disappear FOREVER!”

Opal gasped.

“Even laughter?”

“YES!”

“Music?”

“YES!”

“People talking to their friends?”

“YES!”

Lolo stepped forward.

“Even comforting someone when they're sad?”

Duke paused.

“Well…”

“Even telling someone you love them?” Lolo asked.

Duke looked away.

“THAT'S A VERY LOUD KIND OF FEELING.”

Opal noticed something.

Duke wasn't just grumpy.

He seemed lonely.

But she couldn't let him take away everyone's sounds.

“Why do you want everything quiet so badly?” she asked.

Duke's mustache drooped.

“Because nobody ever lets me sleep. The forest birds sing. The frogs croak. The squirrels chatter. The wind whistles. Even the mushrooms hum!”

He threw his arms into the air.

“I HAVEN'T HAD A PROPER NAP IN FOUR HUNDRED YEARS!”

Langston blinked.

“That's a very long time.”

“I KNOW!”

Duke's eyes filled with sleepy tears.

“And whenever I ask everyone to be quiet, they just laugh!”

Opal looked at Lolo.

She understood a little better now.

Duke wanted peace and quiet, but he had chosen a terrible way to get it.

“You could have asked us for help,” Opal said.

“I DIDN'T WANT TO ASK!”

“Why?”

“BECAUSE I'M A DANGEROUS VILLAIN!”

Langston raised his hand.

“I'm also very dangerous, and I ask people for snacks all the time.”

“LANGSTON!” Opal whispered.

“What? It's good advice.”

Duke stomped his feet.

“ENOUGH! THE MACHINE IS ALMOST READY!”

The purple pillow-heart began glowing.

The machine rumbled.

The friends had to act quickly.

Opal studied the machine.

The stolen sounds were traveling through glowing pipes into the giant pillow.

“Axey, can you reach those little switches?”

Axey nodded.

“Coatl, can you hold the machine steady?”

Coatl planted his feet.

“Finley and Stella, look for a way to open the sound chamber.”

The two doodle girls ran around the machine, sniffing every corner.

“Langston, distract Duke!”

Langston grinned.

“MY SPECIALTY.”

He stepped forward.

“HEY, DUKE! I HAVE A QUESTION!”

“What?”

“DO YOU KNOW HOW TO DO THE PUMPKIN TROLL DANCE?”

Duke blinked.

“The what?”

Langston began dancing.

He wiggled his knees, swung his arms, and spun around in a circle.

Then he slipped on a pillow.

“PLOOOF!”

He landed on his back.

Duke burst out laughing.

It was the first genuine laugh anyone had heard from him.

And when he laughed, a little golden sound floated out of his belly.

Opal noticed immediately.

“Wait! His laughter is releasing the stolen sounds!”

Lolo smiled.

“Maybe the sounds don't belong in his belly. Maybe they want to go home.”

Opal understood.

She reached toward the machine and placed her hand on the glowing pillow-heart.

Her own little gemstone began to shine.

A warm golden light spread through the machine.

The stolen sounds began vibrating.

The giggles bounced.

The songs hummed.

The sleepy sighs floated.

Axey flipped the switches.

Coatl held the machine steady.

Finley and Stella pulled open the sound chamber.

And Langston continued dancing.

“COME ON, DUKE! YOU CAN DO IT!”

Duke tried to resist.

But then he began tapping one foot.

Then the other.

Then his enormous arms started moving.

And suddenly—

“BOOOOOOM!”

The giant pillow-heart burst open in a shower of golden sparkles.

Thousands of stolen sounds floated into the air.

Laughter returned to the forest.

Birds sang.

Fountains splashed.

The wind whistled.

And Langston's snore returned.

“HOOOOONK-SHOOOOO!”

“HEY!” Langston shouted. “I WASN'T READY!”

Everyone laughed.

Duke Dozington sat in the middle of the broken machine.

His nightcap had fallen over one eye.

“My machine…”

Opal walked over.

“Your machine is broken, but everyone has their sounds back.”

Duke looked at the smiling friends.

“I suppose I went a little too far.”

“A little?” Langston asked.

Lolo gave him a gentle nudge.

“Langston.”

“Fine. A medium amount.”

Duke sighed.

“I just wanted some peace and quiet.”

Opal thought for a moment.

Then she had an idea.

“What if we help you build a special quiet room? Somewhere you can nap whenever you want.”

Duke's eyes widened.

“A quiet room?”

“With soundproof walls,” said Axey.

“And soft pillows,” added Lolo.

“And a giant blanket,” said Stella.

Finley barked happily.

Langston raised his hand.

“And a snack cupboard?”

Opal smiled.

“Sure. A snack cupboard.”

Duke's mustache curled upward.

“For me?”

“For you.”

The giant creature slowly stood up.

“Perhaps I could try being a friend instead of a sound-stealing villain.”

Opal nodded.

“That sounds like a much better idea.”

A few days later, Duke Dozington's new quiet room was finished.

It had soft purple walls, a giant bed, fluffy blankets, and a little sign that read:

“PLEASE KNOCK. NAPPING IN PROGRESS.”

Duke loved it.

He could sleep whenever he wanted, and he no longer needed to steal anyone's sounds.

But there was one problem.

He had become obsessed with Langston's pumpkin troll dance.

Every afternoon, he invited Langston over to teach him new moves.

And every afternoon, Langston invented something even sillier.

One day, Duke tried dancing so hard that his nightcap spun around like a helicopter.

“WHIRRRRRRR!”

He lifted three inches off the ground.

“LOOK!” he shouted. “I CAN FLY!”

Langston gasped.

“THE PUMPKIN TROLL DANCE HAS UNLOCKED A NEW POWER!”

Opal and Lolo watched from the doorway, laughing.

“I'm glad we helped him,” Opal said.

Lolo snuggled closer.

“Me too. Sometimes someone who acts grumpy just needs a little help explaining what they need.”

Opal smiled.

“And sometimes a giant purple pillow monster just needs a nap.”

From inside the room came a tremendous sound.

“HOOOOOOOOONK-SHOOOOOOOO!”

Duke was asleep.

Langston peeked inside.

“That's a very impressive snore.”

Then he whispered:

“I'm going to try to beat it.”

Lolo shook her head.

“Please don't.”

And Stuffy Land enjoyed its happiest, noisiest, most giggly Halloween season ever.

Except, of course, inside Duke Dozington's quiet room.

That room was VERY quiet.

The end.
