Opal and the Concert Beyond the Fence

Opal practiced her song all morning. Lolo listened from a cushion while Langston tested the stage by walking across it very dramatically.

“Strong enough for a troll,” he announced.

“It's a concert,” Opal said.

“A concert should always know.”

Unique Pink hung ribbons. Cinny arranged the seats. Cherryberry checked the tickets at the gate, and the two doodle girls helped Axey carry programs without dropping too many.

When Opal stepped into the lantern light, she felt as bright as her gemstone.

Then a crumpled wrapper landed beside her shoe.

Another struck a cushion. An empty paper cup rolled across the stage.

Opal stopped singing.

Beyond the fence stood a crowd of stuffies who had not come through the gate.

“Why are you ruining my concert?” she shouted.

A few voices shouted back. Nobody listened to anybody. Opal left the stage with her cheeks hot and her song stuck somewhere in her throat.

“They don't even like me,” she told Lolo.

Lolo sat beside her. “I saw something upsetting. I don't know yet why it happened.”

“I know why. They wanted to be mean.”

“Could we ask?”

Opal did not want to ask. She wanted everyone to agree that she was right. Langston sat on the other side of her, unusually quiet.

“I could make a very impressive speech,” he offered. “But Lolo's idea might work better.”

They walked back to the fence with Coatl. Axey stayed near the smaller stuffies, making sure everyone had space.

Opal pointed to the paper cup. “Throwing things hurt my feelings. It also made the stage unsafe. Please stop.”

A small voice answered from the other side.

“We wanted to hear. We couldn't see over the fence.”

“Why didn't you buy tickets?”

“We couldn't afford them.”

The words made Opal stop. She had thought about the stage, the ribbons, and how beautiful her song would sound. She had not thought about what it would feel like to hear the music from outside a locked gate.

“But throwing things didn't help me understand,” she said.

“We know. We're sorry.”

Opal looked at Lolo. Then she sat on a cushion beside the fence so everyone could see her face.

“I'm sorry I shouted without asking what was wrong.”

Together they talked to Cherryberry and Cinny. The tickets helped pay for the decorations and refreshments. They could not simply pretend those costs had disappeared.

Opal opened the little jar where she kept her concert coins.

“I can pay for some extra places,” she said.

Cherryberry added a few coins. Coatl offered lanterns they could reuse, and Unique Pink folded spare programs into more decorations. They made space for every friend who had been waiting outside.

First they cleaned the stage together. Finley found three wrappers beneath a cushion. Stella helped a shy kitty carry the recycling basket. Langston retrieved the paper cup with a grand bow.

“An extremely difficult rescue. The cup has requested no interviews.”

This time the gate stayed open. Beside it Cinny placed a sign: IF YOU NEED A PLACE, ASK US.

Opal went back onto the stage.

“I want to start again,” she said. “With a song everybody can join.”

Lolo sat where Opal could see her. Axey bounced to the rhythm. Soon the concert became a dance party, with friends teaching one another the steps.

Even Swirly joined from the edge of the floor. She and Opal were still working through their own quarrel; one shared song did not fix everything. But when Opal offered her a program, Swirly took it.

That was a start.

At the end Langston asked whether he could have a solo.

“A singing solo?” Opal asked.

“A snack solo.”

“You can have a snack with everybody else.”

He considered this. “An ensemble performance. Excellent.”

The End.
