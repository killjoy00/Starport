The Gigglequake of Stuffy Land

The giant cloud arrived during breakfast and sat on the tallest tower as though it were a chair.

“Too many people caring about things,” it grumbled. “Exhausting.”

It shook one enormous sleeve. A gray wisp drifted into the galaxy tent and brushed Opal's gemstone.

Axey hurried in with a torn map. “Can you help me mend this?”

Opal shrugged. “It doesn't matter.”

Lolo looked up.

Opal always cared about a torn map. Sometimes she cared so much that she repaired the fold before Axey finished explaining it.

Stella nudged a fallen blanket toward her. Again Opal shrugged.

Langston waved a cookie over his head. “I have invented a hat!”

“Fine,” Opal said.

He lowered it. “This is worse than I thought.”

Outside, the cloud laughed. The ground bounced beneath the tent, sending the cups into the air. Coatl caught the tray on his back before it could fall.

“A gigglequake,” Axey said, clinging to a cushion. “A very unhelpful gigglequake.”

The cloud announced that soon nobody would care about anybody. There would be no worries, no arguments, and absolutely no reason to share the best chair.

Lolo took Opal's hand. Opal did not pull away, but she did not squeeze back.

“We'll stay with you,” Lolo whispered. “Even while this feels strange.”

Cinny spread the torn map on the floor. The cloud's gray thread ran from Opal's gemstone to a knot beneath its enormous chair. They needed to reach that knot without shaking the whole tower.

Water Kitty made a soft landing pool. The Dragon Couple circled together above the tower. Nebula Johnny held a moon shield over the village while Unique Pink wove a ladder of bright ribbons.

But every time the ladder touched the cloud, it bounced away.

Langston watched it spring through the air.

“What if,” he said, “we stop asking it to be a ladder?”

He and Frosted Kitty pulled the ribbons across a little bounce cloud drifting near the ground. Axey tested it with one cautious hop.

Boing.

Then a bigger hop.

BOING.

“Very promising!” he called from somewhere above the roof.

The giant cloud leaned forward to watch. Langston bowed.

“Welcome to the world's least organized trampoline show!”

He bounced sideways. Frosted Kitty bounced backward. The cloud laughed so hard that it forgot to guard its knot.

Opal watched Lolo tie a safety ribbon around Axey. The ribbon was too long, and Lolo's paws trembled a little.

Something tugged inside Opal. It was small, but it was there.

“Use the shorter loop,” she said.

Lolo looked at her. “Will you show me?”

Opal reached for the ribbon. As she tied the loop, she remembered Axey mending her explorer bag. She remembered Stella waiting beside her bed. She remembered Lolo staying close on days when nothing felt easy.

Her gemstone warmed. It did not break the cloud's spell by itself. It lit the ribbon in her hands, showing the safe path to the knot.

Axey bounced up carrying Caterpillar Kitty, who anchored them with sticky goo. Together they loosened the gray thread.

The cloud lunged for it, but Langston pointed to his head.

“ONE FINAL TRICK!”

The cookie hat fell into his mouth.

The cloud laughed. Axey pulled. The knot came undone, and the gray wisp dissolved into ordinary mist.

Opal squeezed Lolo's paw so tightly that Lolo laughed too.

“I care,” Opal whispered. “I can feel it again.”

The giant cloud sagged. Without its spell, it looked less enormous.

“Caring makes things complicated,” it muttered.

“Sometimes,” Opal said. “You still can't take it away from people.”

They helped it choose a quiet hill where it could rest without sitting on anybody's home. It returned the stolen sunlight and promised to ask before making another gigglequake.

That night Axey unfolded his repaired map. Opal smoothed the last crease, and Lolo tucked herself beside them.

Langston arrived with another cookie on his head.

“A hat?” Opal asked.

“A very short-lived one.”

The End.
