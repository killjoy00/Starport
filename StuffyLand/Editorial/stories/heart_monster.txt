Opal and the Halloween Heart Monster

On Halloween evening, a new kitty stood beyond the lanterns of Stuffy Land. Her fur was blue and black, and a bright heart shone on her chest.

She was not one of the Aphmau kitties. Nobody knew her name.

“I am Hearts-y,” she announced. “And I have come to collect all the friendship.”

Langston looked inside his snack bag. “I have crackers. Will those do?”

“No.”

She stamped one small paw. The heart on her chest flashed, and she grew into an enormous blue-and-black heart monster. Lantern ribbons fluttered. The smallest pumpkins rolled away.

Axey ducked behind Coatl. “That's quite a costume.”

“I don't think it's a costume,” Opal said.

The monster reached for the glowing decorations above the dance floor. Each time she snatched one, another light went out.

“Now it is mine,” she said. But she sounded no happier.

Nebula Johnny spread a moon shield over the friends. The purple and dark-blue Dragon Couple flew together to guide the little kitties beneath it. Coatl's danger buttons shone under his shell.

Opal stayed beside Lolo.

“She wants friendship,” Opal whispered. “Why is she taking the lights?”

“Maybe she thinks friendship is something you can hold,” Lolo said.

Unique Pink tied a ribbon to a pumpkin and rolled it toward the monster. On it was a message: WOULD YOU LIKE TO TALK?

The monster squinted.

“Is this a trick?”

“No,” Opal said. “But we need you to stop grabbing things.”

Hearts-y tightened her enormous paws. Inside them the lights glimmered faintly.

“Everybody here has somebody,” she said. “I thought if I had enough of your glowing things, I would have somebody too.”

Lolo stepped beside Opal. “Did the things make you feel less lonely?”

The monster stared at her paws.

“No.”

Behind the shield, Langston and Frosted Kitty had been preparing a spectacular distraction involving a saucepan and an imaginary trumpet. Opal held up a hand. Langston lowered the saucepan with surprising care.

They did not need a bigger noise. They needed room to listen.

“You can come to our party,” Opal told Hearts-y. “But first we have to give back the decorations.”

“Will everyone still be angry?”

“Some people might be upset,” Lolo said. “You can help make things right.”

Hearts-y slowly opened one paw. A golden ribbon floated down. Then a lantern. Then all the stolen lights, bobbing like warm little stars.

Water Kitty caught a tipping lantern in a cushion of water. Axey scrambled up the ribbon ladder to hang it again. Finley found the last missing pumpkin, and Stella sat with Hearts-y while the enormous monster began to shrink.

Soon a small blue-and-black kitty stood in the lantern light.

“I'm sorry,” she said.

Cherryberry nodded. “Will you help us straighten the dance floor?”

Hearts-y did. She gathered ribbons, carried cushions, and asked before moving anything. When the work was finished, Opal offered her a place beside the tent.

“You don't have to make the heart on your chest bigger,” Opal said. “You can start by being here.”

Langston finally lifted the saucepan.

“Is it time for my trumpet solo?”

“That isn't a trumpet,” Axey said.

“An important technicality.”

He made such a ridiculous noise that Hearts-y laughed. The heart on her chest glowed softly. This time none of the other lights went out.

Later, when they took the Halloween photograph, Hearts-y asked where to stand.

“Here,” Opal said, making room. Lolo tucked herself close, and the two white doodle girls settled at their feet.

Hearts-y smiled without taking a single thing.

The End.
