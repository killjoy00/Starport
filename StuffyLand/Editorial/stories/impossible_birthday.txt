Opal and the Impossible Birthday

Opal blew out her birthday candle.

Then the candle lit itself again.

Langston looked delighted. “Excellent. Unlimited birthdays.”

A moment later the cake became flour, the ribbons returned to their spools, and Axey's carefully wrapped present unfolded into a map.

“Less excellent,” Langston said.

Above the galaxy tent, a time storm spun like a silver pinwheel. Some moments hurried forward. Others stumbled backward. Stella's bow untied itself three times.

Lolo stayed beside Opal. “Are you all right?”

“I wanted everybody to have a lovely day,” Opal said.

“We're still here.”

A small chest appeared where the cake had been. Its lid read MEMORY TREASURE. Beneath the words were three empty shapes: a circle, a ribbon, and a star.

Axey wanted to explore immediately. Cinny wanted a plan first. They compromised by exploring with a plan, which Axey said was almost as good.

Coatl guided them through the tent while Finley checked which footprints belonged to moments that had already happened. The Dragon Couple flew together above a little whirlwind of unwrapped gifts.

They found the circle in a memory of an afternoon by the cushion pond. Opal had dropped a smooth stone, and Langston had pretended it was a rare troll egg.

In the memory he was still explaining how to hatch it.

“Sit on it for four hundred years,” he said. “Bring snacks.”

Opal laughed. A small golden circle rose from the pond and settled into her hand.

The next path led to a memory of Lolo's ribbon coming loose during a long adventure. Opal remembered tying it again while everyone waited.

“It wasn't an exciting part,” Opal said.

“It mattered to me,” Lolo answered.

Opal touched the knot in the memory. A little ribbon of light curled around her wrist.

The storm grew louder. It offered Opal a glimpse of a perfect birthday: a towering cake, every present wrapped exactly right, and no one spilling anything.

In that perfect picture, everybody stood so still that nobody laughed.

Opal stepped back.

“I don't think I want that one.”

The last shape was a star. Axey checked behind the cushions. Finley examined the chest. Langston looked in the snack basket, twice, in case time had changed its contents.

Nothing.

Opal sat down. “What if we missed the best memory?”

Lolo leaned against her. “What if it hasn't happened yet?”

The time storm had tossed a tiny kitty onto the wrong side of a ribbon whirlpool. Axey could reach her, but his map kept folding backward in his paws.

Opal stood. She asked Coatl to anchor a blanket, the Dragon Couple to hold the corners, and Langston to stop the flying presents with his extremely useful size.

Lolo talked to the frightened kitty while Axey crossed the blanket bridge. Opal's gemstone lit the steady stitches beneath his feet.

When they were safely back, the kitty gave Opal a small, fierce hug.

A golden star appeared between them.

They placed all three shapes into the chest. The time storm slowed, turning once, twice, and then settling into a shower of harmless silver sparks.

The cake returned. It leaned to one side. One ribbon hung crooked. Langston's present had been wrapped with a sock by mistake.

Opal loved all of it.

This time she asked everyone to gather close before she blew out the candle.

“Make a wish,” Axey whispered.

Opal looked at Lolo, at her taller brother trying to hide the sock, at the two dogs and all their friends.

She already knew what she wanted to keep.

The candle went out and stayed out.

Langston waited.

“Are we certain there isn't one more birthday?”

“Next year,” Opal said.

He nodded. “Plenty of time to unwrap the sock.”

The End.
