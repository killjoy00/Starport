Langston and the Sneaky Giant Troll

The first clue was the snack basket. It was sitting on top of the wardrobe, wearing Langston's cape.

The second clue was a sign.

BEWARE THE GIANT TROLL. HE IS VERY SNEAKY.

The third clue was Langston hiding behind a lamp that was much smaller than he was.

Opal folded her arms. “I can see your knees.”

“Those are somebody else's knees.”

Lolo peered around Opal. “They have your socks.”

“A remarkable coincidence.”

Langston sprang out with a magnificent roar. The tent flap shook, Axey dropped his compass, and Frosted Kitty shouted, “THE ANNOYINGS HAVE ARRIVED!”

“We were already here,” Langston whispered.

“It's an entrance,” Frosted Kitty whispered back.

Langston declared himself the Sneaky Giant Troll of Stuffy Land. He would hide treasures, invent impossible riddles, and demand payment in imaginary cookies.

At first it was wonderful. He hid a golden ribbon inside an upside-down bowl. Finley found it in seven seconds. He announced a bridge toll of three silly faces. Stella made one small face that everybody agreed was worth three.

Then Langston moved the cushions while Cinny was building a carefully measured staircase.

Cinny stared at the empty space where her next step should have been.

“Surprise!” Langston boomed.

“It is,” Cinny said. She did not sound pleased.

Frosted Kitty made a wall of thorny magical flowers across Axey's exploration trail. Coatl calmly placed himself between the flowers and the smallest plushies.

Axey turned around with his map drooping.

“I liked the trail the way it was.”

Langston scratched his head. Villains in games were supposed to make adventures exciting. Why did everyone look as though the adventure had become a chore?

“Perhaps,” Lolo said, “we should ask whether everyone still wants to play.”

Langston tried a quiet villain voice. It was still rather loud.

“DO YOU STILL WANT TO PLAY?”

“Some of us do,” Opal said. “Some of us want to finish what we're doing.”

“But I haven't even unveiled the Terrible Tower of Slightly Wobbly Pillows.”

Coatl glanced at the tower. “A smaller tower might be wise.”

They sat together beneath the galaxy tent. Langston took off his pumpkin helmet. Without it, he looked less like a terrible troll and more like a brother trying to make an ordinary afternoon exciting.

“I didn't want anyone to feel bad,” he said.

“I know,” Opal replied. “But moving somebody's work without asking still feels bad.”

Frosted Kitty made her flower wall disappear. Langston carried every cushion back to Cinny, then held her ribbon measure while she rebuilt the staircase.

“Left a little,” Cinny said.

He moved left.

“A little less.”

He moved right.

“Perfect.”

Langston brightened. It turned out helping could involve quite a lot of impressive lifting.

Afterward Opal helped him set up a troll game in an empty corner. They made a starting line, a safe cushion bridge, and a sign saying that anyone could join or stop whenever they liked.

Frosted Kitty made soft ribbon loops instead of thorny flowers. Axey tested the route. Coatl steadied the bridge. Lolo sat with anyone who preferred to watch.

This time the Sneaky Giant Troll demanded that challengers solve a riddle.

“What has four legs, no head, and my snack underneath it?”

“A table,” Finley said.

Langston looked alarmed. “How did you know?”

“Your snack is visible.”

The game ended with everyone who wanted to play reaching the treasure. Langston bowed so deeply that his helmet fell into the basket.

Opal helped him pick it up.

“You can be a very good pretend villain.”

“And a very sneaky one?”

She looked at his knees sticking out behind the basket.

“We can work on that part.”

The End.
